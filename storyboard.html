🌿 The GiftLee Philosophy

"I don't care about a good photo. I care about honesty."— Nan Goldin, as cited by GiftLee

This storyboard is built around one idea: the camera is a guest on this trip, not the host. The goal is truth — of two families in a city they've never been to, at a time they'll never get back.

Before you lift the camera — three questions

  • 1
    Will this shot tell the emotional truth of this moment — or just prove we were here?
  • 2
    If I film this, will I miss being in it? Is that trade worth it?
  • 3
    Is this one of the 7 Hero Shots? If not — does it serve the story, or is it a tourist checkbox?
Core Principles
Honesty over perfection
A slightly blurry shot of a real moment beats a sharp shot of a posed one. Always.
Quiet over busy
GiftLee shoots open, peaceful spaces. Melbourne in winter is perfectly quiet. Use it.
Feeling over coverage
Three shots that make you feel something beat ten shots that document everything.
Live the ending
The Penguin Parade, the Rochford toast, the airport goodbye — be there. The film has enough.

🌿 When to Put the Camera Away

GiftLee's best films are made from what she chose not to film. These moments belong to the trip, not the film.

🐧
Penguin Parade — after sunset, no exceptions
The anticipation footage you already shot IS the film. The darkness holds the penguins.
🌊

👦

🧰 Shot Toolkit — Every Location Needs These

Three shot types minimum = something editable in post. But first — ask the three GiftLee questions.

Establish · Wide
  • Show where we are
  • Include something moving
  • Hold for 8+ seconds
B-roll · Medium
  • The activity happening
  • People in the environment
  • Don't announce the shot
Detail · Close
  • Food, textures, signs, hands
  • f/1.4 or f/1.2 · soft background
  • Osmo for sub-70cm shots
Movement
  • Walk-with: chest height, IBIS on
  • Reveal: start tight, pull back
  • Lock-off: let subject move through
Reaction
  • Faces — not posed
  • Wait for the real expression
  • Kids over adults for authenticity
Ambient Audio
  • 60 sec stationary at each location
  • S9 on ground or railing
  • Don't talk during this take

✂️ Edit Philosophy — GiftLee + D'Avella Method

Let shots breathe, trust the silence, and cut before the viewer is ready. The negative space is where the emotion lives.

Structure
Cold open (12 Apostles) → "3 days earlier…" → Act I (D0–D2) → Act II (D3–D5, peaks at Penguins) → Act III (D6–Departure). One title card only: at the Penguin cut-to-black.
Pacing
8–12 second average shot length. No quick cuts except D3 ocean road energy. Act III slows to 12–15 sec — let the vineyard breathe.
Music
Understated, no drop beats. Instrumental — piano, acoustic guitar, or minimal ambient. No music over the Penguin sequence — ambient audio only.
Colour
Warm, slightly desaturated. The Leica Glow is the visual signature — don't fight it. Melbourne winter light is naturally cool — balance toward amber, not blue.
Silence
Cut to black at Penguin Parade. Hold 3+ seconds. Title card: "Phillip Island, 21 August. No cameras allowed after sunset." Hold 2 sec. Cut to next morning.
What to cut
If you're watching a shot thinking "this proves we were there" — cut it. If you're thinking "this is what it felt like" — keep it.
GiftLee's edit rule: If a slightly blurry moment of a child's genuine laugh is in your footage, it belongs in the film. The technically perfect shot of the same child posed for the camera does not.
